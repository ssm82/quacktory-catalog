How we make ducks

Quality control

Every duck goes through 5 checks (Architect gets an extended protocol):

  1. 1 Visual inspection — no porosity, streaks, or short shots.
  2. 2 Geometry — beak/eyes/feet present, proportions ±2%.
  3. 3 Drop test — Micro/Classic: 5 drops from 1 m onto concrete with no cracks.
  4. 4 Smell test — no “rubbery” smell (sign of poor vulcanisation).
  5. 5 Gravimetry — mass within ±3% of the reference.

Architect additionally receives: ultrasonic inspection of the frame's welded seams, on-site assembly photo protocol.

Duck body

single-piece moulding of vulcanised rubber — food-grade silicone compound (for Micro/Classic) or natural rubber with EPDM additives (for Designer/Mega/Architect).

No plastic inserts, no metal inside, no glued seams.

Eyes — paint, not inserts.

Eye shape (oval/round/position) is invariant. The same base mould relief across every SKU.

Eye colour is invariant. Black pupil + white sclera.

Pigment

Pigment is applied post-vulcanisation by hand, with food-grade silicone dye that resists UV and water.

Custom development

Quacktory Pigment Menu

Custom = picking from a branded palette.

Segment D gets 12 branded Pantone-equivalents (TPM — Quacktory Pigment Menu), into which the mass is tinted BEFORE moulding. This is not “paint on top” (annoys buyers with durability and price) — it's body-through pigment.

Fully arbitrary colour (a third-party Pantone) is a separate conversation as a new formula development. Separate line in the quote, +2 weeks to the lead time, +15% to the price.

Custom geometry (a new duck shape, a brand mascot figure) is a separate project with a master model, discussed individually.

Material certifications

EN 71-3 (chemical migration), REACH (no phthalates or bisphenol A).

Temperature range: −20 °C … +60 °C. Above +70 °C — deformation. Below −25 °C — embrittlement (especially Mega/Architect).

Indoor only. Protect from direct UV (colour degrades after 2+ years) and from oils/solvents.

Care: wash with warm water and mild soap. No abrasives, alcohol, or bleach.
