Universal prompt

One-liner. A high-contrast, dark-mode hero composed as a centered editorial statement, where a single saturated accent on a near-black field does the heavy lifting and everything else is restrained negative space, wide-tracked typography, and a line-based immersive backdrop.

Direction. The mood is cold, precise, technical, and quietly cinematic. The hero strategy is a slow-burning typographic moment that assumes the viewer will pause, absorb, and then make a single primary decision. Emotionally, it is doing the work of a gallery wall — a single loud object in a quiet room — rather than a marketplace. The composition leads the eye down a centered vertical axis and then opens outward into a dense, faintly rendered environment that supplies atmosphere without competing with the focal point.

Color. The palette is built on a two-tone logic: a deep neutral field plus a single saturated accent, and that logic is what must survive any thematic pivot. The background is near-black, sitting in the range of #0A0A0A to #0E0E10, anchoring the entire page as a uniform dark canvas. Surface elevations, when needed, lift only slightly into a charcoal band around #1F1F22 to #2A2A2E, used for linework, dividers, and faint environmental graphics — never for large fills. Foreground body copy reads in a light cool gray near #B8B8BD, deliberately desaturated so it never competes with the accent. The accent itself is a saturated warm yellow in the #F5E020 to #FFE600 band, used sparingly but decisively on the display word, the primary action, a thin vertical marker, and active states. The temperature story is the classic cold-warm contrast: cool dark base, warm yellow focal point, and the brain reads yellow as a tiny sun sitting on a black plane. Contrast logic puts the yellow at maximum punch against the near-black, the body gray comfortably above threshold against the field, and any additional accent (if introduced) must be kept at very low saturation or it will vibrate against the yellow. There is no gradient on text, no glow filter, no glass surface; a very subtle edge vignette is the only allowed ambient tint. If the system is extended, the rule is simple: one saturated accent, used on no more than three element classes, never as a fill behind text.

Typography. One neutral geometric grotesque family does all the work, differentiated by scale, weight, case, and tracking. The display face is a Space Grotesk-class grotesque used at an extreme size — roughly one-third of the viewport width — set in uppercase with very wide letter-spacing so the word behaves as a decorative object as much as legible text. The body face is an Inter-class grotesque set in sentence case at a small but comfortable size, with generous leading around 1.5 to 1.7. The same family, dropped to small uppercase with wide tracking, handles all chrome: top-bar labels, button labels, and the stepper. There is no secondary display face, no serif, and no monospaced font in the visible system; if a numeric or mono role is required later, it should be a quiet utility, never a second voice. The scale ratio between display and body is aggressive — roughly 8:1 to 12:1 — which is what gives the layout its editorial weight. Weight stays regular to medium; nothing is bold, and emphasis is built through size, tracking, and surrounding negative space rather than through stroke. The display word's line-height is tight; the paragraph's line-height is loose. All-caps treatments always carry wide tracking; body copy never does. The single typographic rule to preserve: the display word is the main visual accent, and its scale carries more compositional weight than its color.

Layout and composition. The page is a centered, symmetrical hero with a one-column central stack and quiet flanking elements. Vertically, the upper half is dominated by negative space; the lower half is filled by an immersive line-based environment. The central content occupies roughly the middle third of the viewport horizontally and reads in a strict top-to-bottom stack: a small top chrome strip, then display word, then paragraph, then action, then a wide lower zone of environmental graphics. The grid underneath is a standard 12-column system with content centered around the midline, and horizontal alignment between the top-left mark, the top-center stepper, and the top-right menu. Proportions of the page read as approximately 8% top chrome, 35% upper negative space with centered content, 50% immersive lower environment, and 7% bottom breathing room; these are flexible, but the airy-on-top, dense-on-bottom split is non-negotiable. Negative space is the primary design tool in the upper half and the reason the focal point reads instantly. Asymmetry is minor: a thin vertical accent bar on the upper left, a circular directional control on the right edge, and a spire-like graphic in the lower right counterbalance each other around the central axis.

Visual hierarchy. The eye hits the display word first because of its scale and isolation. Second, the body paragraph registers as supporting context directly underneath. Third, the primary action — the pill button in saturated accent — anchors the decision. Fourth, the upper-left vertical accent bar and the right-edge circular control register as supporting wayfinding. Fifth and last, the top-bar labels and stepper numerals read as chrome. Emphasis is built through scale, surrounding empty space, and saturated hue. Legibility is near-instant because there are only three vertical tiers in the central stack. The single risk to this hierarchy is the brightest node in the lower environment, which can pull attention away from the headline if it sits too close to the focal column; that node should always be dimmed or removed.

Hero treatment. The hero is a fixed-camera composition: a deep black void above, a single large typographic object at center, a short paragraph, a single action, and a faintly rendered architectural line-art environment filling the lower half like a museum tableau. The display word is framed by wide empty space on all sides, never crowded by supporting graphics. The headline is not a sentence but a word-as-object, set wide and uppercase so it reads as a label, a title, or a brand mark rather than as a call to think. The supporting paragraph sits directly below, sentence case, small, low-emphasis, never competing with the display. The action sits below the paragraph, in a pill shape, fully saturated. The lower environment reads as depth rather than content: thin lines, low opacity, monochrome, suggesting a city, a skyline, a circuit, or a constellation depending on the topic — but always as faint scaffolding behind the central statement. There is no photographic plate, no video background, and no gradient mask over the transition; the airiness of the upper half does the separation work.

Navigation and wayfinding. The primary nav is a thin top strip: a small mark on the left, a quiet stepper in the center (formatted as two numerals with a long dash, e.g. 01 — 02), and a text link plus hamburger on the right. The secondary nav is a circular outlined directional control pinned to the right edge, sized large enough to be a tap target but never competing with the central CTA. There are no breadcrumbs, no tab strips, no sidebars, and no floating menus on the hero. The stepper behaves as a position indicator rather than a control set; the right-edge circle behaves as a next/previous affordance; the hamburger behaves as a full menu escape hatch. All three secondary cues should share a single stroke weight and a single minimum radius, and none of them should ever use the saturated accent unless in an active state.

Media and imagery. Photography is absent. Imagery is built from linework, geometric primitives, and one flat saturated accent. The dominant graphic is a faint architectural or schematic line drawing that fills the lower half, rendered in a charcoal that sits only one or two values above the background — the rule is that it should be perceptible but never assertive. There is no bloom, no grain, no duotone, no halftone, and no photographic texture; the only filter in use is a low-opacity, low-saturation treatment on the linework itself. Where real images are introduced in later sections, they should sit on a near-black surface and be allowed one piece of supporting yellow at most — the environment should not have to fight the focal object for the accent. Illustrations, if any, follow the same flat, geometric, monochrome-with-accent rule as the hero.

Standout solutions. First, the single-accent-on-black rule: reserve the saturated yellow for no more than three element classes (the display word, the primary action, and an active or wayfinding marker), and never use it as a fill behind text. Second, the display-word-as-object: a single grotesque word set in wide-tracked uppercase at extreme scale, carrying the composition through typographic mass rather than color. Third, the centered vertical reading stack: display, paragraph, action, in that order, with generous leading and a single column. Fourth, the top chrome triplet: mark left, stepper center, menu right, all set in tiny uppercase with wide tracking — a quiet gallery-label system rather than a navigation bar. Fifth, the right-edge circular directional control as a soft secondary cue, never louder than the CTA. Sixth, the immersive lower-half line environment: full-bleed, low-opacity, monochrome, functioning as atmosphere rather than content.

Things to learn from. Let one element carry the color story; do not spread the accent across the page. Use negative space in the upper half as a premium signal and let the lower half supply density. Make the display word do double duty as both the brand voice and the layout anchor. Tie every interactive node to a labeled verb or numeral so chrome never has to be decoded. Keep the entire motion vocabulary to opacity, small translate-up, and restrained scale, all on a single ease-out curve.

Watch-outs. The brightest node in the lower environment can pull the eye away from the headline and should be dimmed or moved. A right-edge directional control with too little weight will read as decoration; give it a minimum tap target and consider an active-state color shift. Paragraph text set small and gray-on-black is the most marginal element for contrast and should be tested at body sizes, not just at scale. Two greens of similar value, or any second saturated hue near the yellow, will cause color vibration; never introduce a competing warm. A top-center stepper that is too quiet becomes useless as orientation; keep numerals legible. Icons without labels will be ambiguous in this restrained palette; pair every glyph with a word. Focal-point crowding happens fast when the lower environment creeps too high; preserve the empty band above the display word. Reduced-motion users must still receive the core hierarchy, so any parallax, letter-reveal, or scale-up animation needs an opacity-only fallback.

Animation. The motion personality is smooth, inertial, and minimal. The first screen fades the display word in with a 12 to 20 pixel translate-up over 600 to 900 milliseconds, optionally word-by-word, then brings the paragraph in 300 milliseconds later, then the button in another 200 milliseconds later with a small scale from 0.96 to 1. The top chrome fades in immediately or within the first 200 milliseconds. The lower environment drifts continuously at roughly 0.3 to 0.5 pixels per frame to suggest depth without distraction; the upper-left vertical accent bar stays static as an anchor. Scroll-driven reveals are limited to a soft fade plus 16 to 24 pixel translate-up, staggered 60 to 100 milliseconds between siblings, with subtle parallax between foreground and midground layers of the line environment. Pinning is avoided because it fights the airy editorial pacing. Hover and micro-interactions are restrained: the button scales to 1.03 with a brightness shift over about 180 milliseconds, the circular control scales to 1.08 and shifts its stroke to the accent on hover, links animate an underline offset over about 160 milliseconds, and the active stepper numeral shifts from gray to the accent. Effects are never stacked — one transform and one color shift at most. Technical rules: use transform and opacity, never animate blur across large areas, never move body text, and respect prefers-reduced-motion by disabling parallax and shortening all reveals to opacity-only fades. Easing is a single curve family: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) for entrances, cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) for state changes, and a soft spring only on the primary action.

Visual scenario. The interface is built for a thoughtful, single-decision entry moment on a wide desktop canvas. The session is short — the screen is a gateway, not a workspace — and the page tolerates a strong opening statement because there is only one thing to do. On smaller screens the centered reading order, the display-word prominence, the accent role, the action accessibility, and the contrast ratios must be preserved; below 768 pixels the lower environment should become denser and lower in opacity, the display word should scale fluidly with a clamp function, the top chrome should collapse to mark plus menu, and paragraph line length should hold near 60 to 70 characters.

Summary. A high-contrast dark hero on a single-accent palette, organized as a centered editorial composition with one large wide-tracked display word, a short paragraph, a single saturated pill action, and a faint line-art environment filling the lower half. The system runs on typographic scale and negative space; the accent is used sparingly; the chrome is gallery-quiet; the motion vocabulary is opacity, small translate-up, restrained scale, and one ease-out curve. What must survive any thematic pivot: the two-tone color logic, the centered reading order, the generous upper-half breathing room, the display word as compositional anchor, and the single primary action.
