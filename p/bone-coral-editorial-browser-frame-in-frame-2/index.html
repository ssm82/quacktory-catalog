Universal prompt

One-liner — an editorial, high-contrast, single-accent interface system built on a strict typographic grid that earns its drama from one expressive organic shape per screen, framed by a theatrical dark environment that focuses attention inward.

Direction — the overall mood is composed, confident, and quietly expressive, registering as a staged object rather than a casual layout. The emotional register is restrained but bold at the system level, neutral at the element level, meaning the page can carry a single loud color field or accent while every component around it remains calm and information-first. The hero strategy is asymmetric and modular: a left-weighted oversized headline pivots against a compact upper-right call-to-action, while one dominant central visual acts as a sculptural counterweight that breaks the orthogonal grid on purpose. The page rewards a few seconds of attention rather than supporting quick scanning, so the first screen alone must carry the entire message without leaning on supplementary content above the fold.

Color — the palette is neutral with a single saturated accent, temperature-mixed so the interior of the screen reads warm while the surrounding environment reads cool. The primary surface inside the canvas is a warm paper off-white in the neighborhood of #F5F4EE, the foreground ink is a near-black close to #0E0E0E, and the supporting surface is pure white around #FFFFFF. A muted cool blue-gray acts as a secondary text and ambient tone in the range of #6E7A86, while a deep ink blue near #1B1F26 functions as the theatrical environment backdrop that frames the canvas from outside. The single saturated accent is a warm red-orange in the neighborhood of #E23B2C, used exclusively on the brand mark, the primary call-to-action fill, and a single small motif inside the central visual — never on body copy, never on surfaces, and never as a background field. Contrast logic is binary and severe: maximum-contrast text on the paper surface, with the accent reserved for the moments that need to win attention. The dark environment around the canvas is not a separate page color but a framing device that pushes the eye toward the lit interior; under-glow or brand-tinted accent light should appear only on the underside of the central sculptural element, in a soft desaturated wash of the same red-orange, to tie the focal piece back to the accent token. When extending the system, red-orange stays on logo, primary action, and one signature motif — adding a second saturated color immediately erodes the discipline.

Typography — three families do all the work, separated by role rather than by display. The display face is Space Grotesk, set bold at roughly 96–160 pixels on desktop (occupying about a third of viewport height and two-thirds of viewport width in the hero), tight leading, tight letter-spacing, sentence case, and high x-height. The body face is Inter at roughly 16–18 pixels with a generous leading of around 1.5–1.6, set in regular or light weight, and used for all supporting copy, captions, and metadata. The mono face is JetBrains Mono, used only for small chip-like metadata, index markers, dates, counts, and any tracked all-caps utility labels where a data or technical register is appropriate. Hierarchy is built almost entirely through weight and scale rather than through typeface switching — the bold Space Grotesk display carries the page, the Inter body carries the information, and the JetBrains Mono labels carry the system markers. Tracked all-caps treatment is reserved for the small mono labels and for the call-to-action label; both should sit at roughly 11–12 pixels with wide tracking around 0.08–0.12em. The scale ratio between display and body should feel deliberately oversized, often 6× to 10×, so the headline functions as the primary visual accent on the page rather than as mere title copy.

Layout & Composition — the system uses an asymmetric, modular grid with a left-weighted heading and a counterbalanced accent on the upper right. The primary zones are: a top zone of roughly 70% viewport height containing the headline, the central visual, and the upper-right call-to-action, followed by a lower band of roughly 30% containing small utility blocks and a media card. Side margins are premium and breathing, typically 6–10% of viewport width, with internal gutters consistent across the layout. The primary visual focus sits roughly on the vertical centerline but visually anchors the middle of the screen, acting as a pivot between the oversized left headline and the compact right-side action. Negative space is the dominant structural material — separation comes from whitespace alone, not from borders, dividers, or shadows. The lower row of utility blocks follows a strict modular rhythm in which each block is separated by spacing only and reads as a tile within a horizontal strip; this rhythm should hold a clear tokenized scale (e.g., units of 80, 160, 240, 320 pixels) rather than ad-hoc dimensions. Focal point placement favors the geometric center of the canvas, but the eye is led there via the headline's right edge rather than via the headline's center, so the central visual feels like the natural resolution of a left-to-right reading path.

Visual Hierarchy — the eye should hit, in order: first the oversized three-line headline anchored by scale and weight, second the central organic visual isolated in white space and tinted with the red-orange accent, third the upper-right call-to-action which wins on color saturation, fourth the small lower-left utility block which wins on positional balance, and fifth the lower-right media card which functions as a teaser. The headline wins on scale, the central visual wins on uniqueness, the call-to-action wins on color, the lower-left tile wins on positional counterweight, and the media card deliberately loses by being quieted so it does not pull against the central piece. There should be no competing accent on the page — the red-orange appears in exactly three places (logo, call-to-action fill, and one motif on the central visual) and nowhere else, and the lower-right media block should be tonally subdued so it does not compete with the centerpiece.

Hero Treatment — the hero is a single fixed-camera composition functioning like a museum tableau, with a near-black ambient environment surrounding a clean off-white canvas, and on that canvas one dominant sculptural 3D or photographic element floating freely without a frame, a card, or a mask. The headline sits to the left, scaled to dominate the upper third, in bold Space Grotesk, sentence case, with a single deliberate line break rhythm. The call-to-action sits to the upper right as a compact filled red-orange button with a gentle radius, a small leading icon, a dot separator, and a short uppercase label. The central visual is treated as a sculpture: isolated, scaled large, surrounded by generous negative space, and given a soft red-orange under-glow that ties it back to the accent token. The lower band of small utility blocks and a single media card acts as a typographic foundation — flat, quiet, separated by whitespace. Framing comes from the dark environment outside the canvas, which functions as a stage edge rather than as a separate page section.

Navigation & Wayfinding — primary navigation is minimal, integrated into the top-right of the canvas as a single compact call-to-action rather than as a full multi-item bar. Secondary navigation, where it exists, sits as a small mono label or utility chip and reads as system metadata rather than as a menu strip. Scrollytelling drivers, if used, are subtle: a faint scroll cue, a small arrow icon, or a section number, each with a clear affordance so it does not feel ambiguous. Ghost / pill / strip UI chrome is restrained — only the primary action wears a filled pill shape; everything else is flat text on the paper surface, separated by whitespace alone.

Media & Imagery — photography, 3D, and illustration are used as sculptural focal points rather than as decorative texture. Real images, when they appear, sit inside a single large rectangular or square block with a small caption, treated as a supporting teaser rather than as a primary element. The dominant media piece is rendered as a standalone object on the paper surface — not cropped into a frame, not given a card, not masked — and floats in white space with a soft directional shadow. Render style is matte and softly lit, with a desaturated palette that allows the red-orange accent to remain the loudest note on the page. Any photographic or illustrative treatment should avoid bloom, grain, duotone, or color overlays that would compete with the paper feel; if a tonal treatment is needed, it should be a soft desaturated wash that keeps the image in the same value range as the surrounding text. Empty outlined rectangles may appear as scaffolding in supporting sections, with very thin, low-contrast hairlines, to make the underlying modular grid visible without competing with content.

Standout Solutions — the most replicable moves are these. First, a single saturated accent reserved for three fixed roles — logo mark, primary call-to-action fill, and one signature motif on the central visual — so the accent becomes a mnemonic that always points the eye to the most important element on any screen. Second, a theatrical dark environment framing a clean off-white canvas, so every interior page reads as a lit object on a stage, raising perceived quality without adding layout complexity. Third, an oversized display headline set in a single weight that wins hierarchy through scale alone, allowing the body face and the mono face to remain quiet and consistent. Fourth, an organic central visual that deliberately breaks the orthogonal grid, so each screen carries one expressive moment while everything else stays strict and architectural. Fifth, a horizontal utility strip across the bottom of the hero in which each block is separated by whitespace alone, with one block functioning as a small media card to ground the composition. Sixth, soft under-glow lighting on the central visual in a desaturated wash of the accent color, so the focal element is tied back to the brand token without introducing a new hue.

Things to Learn From — let the single accent carry the color story and apply it to no more than three fixed roles; integrate the typographic system as the page's primary visual element so the design is legible even before any imagery loads; pair every interactive node with a labeled verb or a small leading icon so affordance is never ambiguous; treat the central visual as a sculpture and protect its negative space rather than crowding it with supporting elements; build the layout as a tokenized modular grid so the system extends without losing rhythm; resist the temptation to add borders, shadows, or dividers when whitespace alone can do the work.

Watch-Outs — a single saturated accent used in too many roles will fragment the eye and turn the page into a colored grid rather than a composition; competing colors of similar value, especially two greens or two blues sitting near each other in supporting imagery, will create a vibrating distraction that breaks the discipline of the palette; counters, stats, and numeric labels without units, dates, or context will read as decorative rather than informative and should always be paired with a small mono caption; small utility blocks and metadata text set below 12–13 pixels will fail accessibility at common viewing distances and need a small bump; icon-only controls without labels will be ambiguous — always pair an icon with a word; focal-point crowding around the central visual, where supporting elements, captions, and badges all converge, will collapse the breathing room that gives the centerpiece its drama; a media card in the lower band that carries the same color weight as the central visual will compete for attention and should be quieted through desaturated treatment and reduced scale; motion that scales the headline, applies blur to the central visual, or uses spring overshoot will break the editorial register and should be confined to opacity and small translate only.
