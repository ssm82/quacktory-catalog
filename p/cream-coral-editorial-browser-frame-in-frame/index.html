Universal prompt

A soft, painterly, single-screen landing composition built on a contained rounded frame floating over a watercolor background, anchored by a single centered lowercase wordmark and a single dark pill button.

The design is trying to produce an airy, imaginative, dreamy first impression — a moment of emotional pause rather than a scanning or browsing experience. The hero strategy is a "world inside the page" effect: one large rounded panel sits at the geometric center of the canvas, holding an illustrated or photographic tableau, and the entire surrounding page acts as a quiet textured mat. There is no navigation, no secondary content, no competing calls to action, no supporting deck of body copy — the eye is led to a single focal point and a single decision. The emotional register is editorial and unhurried, achieved through soft watercolor edges, pastel mid-tones, a generous breathing zone between the frame and the page edges, and two warm-toned organic elements that cross the frame boundary from opposite corners to give the composition humanity without breaking its calm.

The palette is built on a cool-neutral base warmed only by a small set of skin-toned and pink accents. The outer page background is a muted teal or seafoam, roughly #7CB8A6 with watercolor variation drifting toward #A8D5C3. The interior of the central frame is a sky-to-surface gradient running from a very pale blue #D9E8EE at the top to an off-white #F2F7F9, with a cool pale green water or ground band around #C8DDD0. Inside the scene, mid-ground vegetation, architecture, or set pieces sit at a lavender or mauve around #A38AC2 with warm earth browns around #8A6A4A. Two organic elements — limbs, branches, hands, ribbons, tendrils, whatever fits the thematic pivot — introduce a soft green around #7FBF6E and a warm beige or peach around #D4A78A. Floating decorative shapes scattered through the scene are a soft pink around #F4B6C8. The central wordmark is set in a near-black #1F2226, and the primary interactive button is a dark charcoal #1C1F23 with white type. The role logic is strict: the teal watercolor is the field, the sky gradient is the surface, the lavender and green are mid-ground, the peach and pink are the only true accents, and the near-black is reserved exclusively for the wordmark and the single primary action. Contrast is moderate across the scene, but the dark pill button is intentionally the highest-contrast element on the page — it must read as the only clearly clickable object. There is no under-glow, no glass blur, no neon halo, no rim light. The system gains its depth from soft gradients, atmospheric haze, and watercolor bleed at the frame edges, not from luminance tricks.

Typography is held to a single geometric, slightly humanist sans-serif with soft terminals and rounded counters — Manrope or an equivalent family such as DM Sans, Public Sans, or a tuned Nunito Sans. No second family is introduced. The display weight is semibold to bold, set at roughly twelve to fourteen percent of viewport width — large enough to read as a hero mark, small enough to feel like a wordmark or logotype rather than a headline. The body weight is regular and is used only inside the dark pill label, the status chips, and the micro indicators. A tracked all-caps treatment is not used anywhere; the entire system is lowercase or sentence case. A mono or numeric face is not required. The scale ratio between the display word and the next-largest text (the button label) is roughly four to one, and between the button label and the smallest indicators roughly three to one. Letter-spacing on the centered word is slightly tightened — minus one to minus two percent — to give it a unified logotype feel; letter-spacing on small chip text is slightly opened at plus four to plus six percent. The wordmark sits on a single line. There are no multi-line hero headlines and no supporting deck of body copy placed directly adjacent to it on the first screen.

The first-screen layout is a single centered hero with no nav, no header bar of substance, and no footer. The rounded frame occupies roughly seventy-five to eighty percent of the page width and is anchored vertically with about twelve percent top and bottom negative space and six to eight percent left and right. The grid is not a strict column grid; the alignment principle is center-anchored with symmetrical vertical axes. The frame is the largest single object on screen, and everything around it is quiet. Negative space is treated as a premium material, used to isolate the framed media as a contained world. The only deliberate break in symmetry is the diagonal axis created by the two organic elements entering from opposite corners and extending outside the frame, partially clipped at the page edges. This diagonal is the tension device of the composition; it must be preserved in any thematic pivot even when the specific organic motif is swapped.

The visual hierarchy is strictly linear and must not be diluted. The eye hits the centered lowercase wordmark first because of its central position, dark weight, and contrast against the brightest part of the sky gradient. The framed media block is the second hit as the largest object on the screen. The dark pill button at the bottom edge of the frame is the third hit and the highest-contrast solid element. The top-edge hairline tick marks with two small numeric values are the fourth hit, visible but visually quiet. The upper-right translucent status pill is the fifth and final hit — the smallest and lowest-contrast element, present as ambient feedback rather than as an actionable target. There is no secondary CTA, no secondary type cluster, and no competing accent that would split the reading path.

The hero is a single, fixed, wide-aspect composition functioning as a contained tableau inside a rounded rectangle. Inside the frame is a stylized scene — illustrated, photographic, or three-d rendered — that reads as a complete small world: a sky-to-ground gradient, mid-ground vegetation or architecture, and the two warm-toned organic elements reaching in from opposite corners. The frame has a large uniform corner radius in the twenty-four to thirty-two pixel range, a near-invisible thin light edge, and a soft diffuse drop shadow beneath it that gives a slight floating-card feel without heaviness. The headline role is held entirely by the centered wordmark, which sits directly over the brightest sky band and reads as a graphical object inside the scene rather than as a heading above it. Below the wordmark, at the bottom edge of the frame, sits a pill-shaped dark button with a leading circular icon and white text, paired with a secondary circular icon-only button to its right. The button cluster is the only interactive layer. Top-edge hairline ticks and an upper-right translucent pill function as ambient indicators and must never compete for attention. The motion language is calm and inertial: the frame reveals with a soft scale-from-center and opacity fade over roughly one thousand to twelve hundred milliseconds on an ease-out curve, the petals or floating shapes drift once and stop, the wordmark fades in with a twelve to eighteen pixel upward translate at six hundred to nine hundred milliseconds after the frame, and the button arrives last at around nine hundred milliseconds. Hover lifts are restricted to a one to two pixel Y-translate with a subtle shadow growth over one hundred fifty to two hundred fifty milliseconds; springs, overshoots, color shifts, and continuous ambient motion are all inappropriate for this mood. Parallax, if used at all, is capped at roughly eight percent translate on the watercolor background layer and is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion, where reveals collapse to opacity-only fades at two hundred milliseconds.

There is no primary navigation on the first screen — the design treats this as an entry moment, not a browsing moment. There is no secondary nav, no breadcrumbs, no sticky chrome. Scrollytelling is not appropriate for the first screen; if subsequent screens exist, they should reveal with a small translate-Y and fade, with stagger of sixty to ninety milliseconds across repeating rows. Ghost chrome — translucent pills, hairline ticks — is allowed only as ambient status feedback, never as a navigation surface.

The central panel is where the visual story lives. The render style is soft, painterly, low-contrast, and slightly textured — watercolor edges, pastel mid-tones, atmospheric haze in the upper portion of the frame, and a faint vignette. If photography is used, it should be heavily desaturated and warmed to sit inside the pastel register; if three-d, the lighting should be diffuse and global with no specular highlights or rim glow; if illustration, the brushwork should be loose with visible bleed at edges. Floating accent shapes — petals, leaves, confetti, sparks, whatever fits the domain — drift across the scene at low opacity. A duotone or grain pass is acceptable but must be subtle; bloom is not appropriate. Real images, if any, sit inside the frame; the outer page is a watercolor-textured paper surface, not photography.

Several moves are worth replicating as reusable patterns in any thematic pivot. First, the contained rounded frame floating over a watercolor background — the core "world inside the page" device — should be preserved; the frame does double duty as image container, card, and hero all at once, and these roles should not be split. Second, a single centered lowercase wordmark acting as both logo and visual anchor should sit inside the scene, not above it, and should never be paired with a multi-line headline on the same screen. Third, the diagonal axis created by two organic elements breaking the frame from opposite corners is the tension device that prevents the composition from feeling sterile; replace the specific motif with whatever fits the domain but keep the principle of two warm-toned asymmetric entries that cross the frame boundary. Fourth, a strict radius family — one large value for the frame, 999px for all pills, 50% for circular icons — should be applied uniformly across every chrome element. Fifth, a single dark pill button should be the only clearly clickable object, with a paired secondary circular icon button that shares the same hover-state language. Sixth, ambient micro-indicators — top-edge hairline ticks, upper-right translucent status pills — should signal system state without competing for attention and should be treated as decorative chrome rather than navigation.

Let one element carry the entire color story and do not scatter accents. Integrate any timeline or sequence logic into the bottom band of the frame, not as a separate section. Pair every interactive node with a labeled verb — an icon plus a short word — not with an icon alone. Treat the hero as a single tableau rather than a stack of components, since the frame is the page. Choose one moment of focus per screen and remove everything that competes with it. Use the contrast between a cool base and a single warm accent as the primary palette logic, and do not introduce a second accent temperature.

Several traps are worth flagging in advance. Color vibration appears at busy intersections where the pink accent meets the lavender foliage; pull one toward a more saturated or desaturated value so they read as distinct. Two greens of similar value — foliage against the organic element — can collapse into one muddy mass; offset them by hue and lightness. The centered wordmark sits over the brightest part of the sky and is borderline legible there, so add a soft inner shadow or a gentle bottom darkening inside the frame, or move the wordmark slightly down into a calmer zone. Small assets — the secondary circular button, the upper-right pill — fall below comfortable touch targets on mobile and feel underweight against the main pill; give the circular button a stronger fill or border. The top-edge hairline ticks become noise below roughly 640px and should be hidden. The diagonal limb composition collapses on narrow viewports, so the mobile layout should not force the symmetry; redesign as a vertical stack with the wordmark above the button and the organic elements reduced to corner accents. Continuous ambient animation of the floating decorative shapes will kill the calm mood, so they should drift once at first paint and then stop. The wordmark, the watercolor background, and the centered focal element must never animate on scroll; all motion should be reserved for entrances and hover states, and prefers-reduced-motion should collapse every reveal to an opacity-only fade at 200ms.
